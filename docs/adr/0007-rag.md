# ADR 0007: Why RAG

## Status
Accepted

## Decision
Ground AI responses/generated material in the student's own uploaded sources via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (embeddings + retrieval + NotebookLM), rather than relying on the base model's parametric knowledge alone.

## Context
The core product value proposition (`docs/PROJECT_PLAN.md#core-value-proposition`) is grounded, citable tutoring from the student's *own* material — a plain "chat with an LLM" product would be generic, uncitable, and prone to hallucination relative to the student's actual coursework.

## Alternatives Considered
- **Fine-tuning per student/course** — impractical to fine-tune a model per student/notebook; far more expensive and slower to update than retrieval, and doesn't naturally support citation.
- **Long-context stuffing (send the whole document every time)** — simpler, but doesn't scale to multi-document notebooks and wastes tokens (`docs/TOKEN_OPTIMIZATION.md`) on irrelevant content; also loses NotebookLM's cross-document reasoning benefit.

## Tradeoffs
RAG adds retrieval-pipeline complexity (chunking, embeddings, ranking — `docs/AI.md#rag-design`) compared to naive context-stuffing, and retrieval quality directly bounds answer quality (bad chunking/ranking = bad answers even with a strong model).

## Consequences
Every document ingested must be chunked and embedded before it's usable for grounded generation (`docs/AI_WORKFLOWS.md#1-upload-document--notebooklm--gemini--notes`); citation metadata must be preserved end-to-end from chunk to generated output.
